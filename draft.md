# MTC Document Validation — draft 01

Status: independent application draft. Normative keywords describe this proposed profile, not additional requirements of the IETF MTC specification.

## 1. Problem and scope

A document may need verifiable signatures after its signing certificate expires or its issuer retires. Certificate issuance, online service availability and document retention have different lifetimes. This draft specifies the retained inputs and validation decisions for that setting. It does not change MTC certificate bytes, introduce an archive-signature object, define a CA governance hierarchy, or replace an AdES container standard.

The selected MTC baseline is [draft-ietf-plants-merkle-tree-certs-07](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-plants-merkle-tree-certs-07.txt), an Internet-Draft published on 7 October 2026. It assigns PKIX identifiers and permits unknown GREASE cosignatures. [Revision 08](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-plants-merkle-tree-certs-08.txt) was published on 2 November 2026; its change log states that revisions 07 and 08 have compatible wire formats. A deployment MUST select an exact revision and verifier; this draft retains revision 07 as its selected source. The retained editor snapshot is a historical comparison. The selected signed-artifact starting point remains [draft-ietf-plants-mtc-deploy-use-cases-00](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-plants-mtc-deploy-use-cases-00.txt), an informational WG Internet-Draft published on 23 October 2026. [Revision 01](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-plants-mtc-deploy-use-cases-01.txt) was published on 2 November 2026 and changes the archival guidance described in section 5 below. This draft adds a document retention horizon, authority/status history, proof closure and explicit renewal deadlines. The earlier correction to the landmark row bound is not a new proposal here.

## 2. Inputs and separate conclusions

The verifier receives original document and signature bytes, an identified application profile, the validation state time, the knowledge time, and externally admitted trust and algorithm policies. Trust MUST NOT be bootstrapped from certificates, checkpoints or policy objects supplied only by the document.

The result distinguishes cryptographic integrity, certificate authorization at the relevant time, preservation continuity, and present admissibility. It records both times and missing dependencies. A historically valid result does not establish current acceptance. A later known compromise may invalidate a signature at an earlier state time according to the applicable policy.

The state time MUST be justified by the selected application's evidence and time policy; it is not inferred from a claimed signing time. The knowledge time is the cutoff for facts admitted to the historical decision. A verifier MUST report the cutoff it actually evaluated and MUST NOT silently substitute its current clock or an archived policy object's preferred cutoff.

Identity qualification, signing-key possession and authorization for the particular document are separate checks. The selected application identifies their evidence and authority. Login, identity-credential presentation, an issuance inclusion proof and a timestamp are not interchangeable evidence for these claims. This draft does not prescribe a new identity or intent object.

## 3. Retained evidence closure

For self-contained distribution, the signer SHOULD select standalone MTC representation before constructing the signature. Preserve the exact TBSCertificate, proof, extensions, leaf position, inclusion path and required signatures. Retain the corresponding issuer/operator keys, accepted membership and scope, and the policy authenticating their authority. A change from revision-06 placeholder OIDs to revision-07 assigned OIDs changes the issuer name and Merkle leaf commitment. Replacing identifiers in retained certificates is not preservation or migration. Evidence renewal MUST preserve the original bytes and exact verification interpretation; an implementation that no longer supports that revision MUST NOT claim to have validated it under a successor.

A relative certificate requires an explicit preservation contract for its authenticated landmark, log/range scope, epoch, policy and all proof dependencies. A live cache or issuer URL is insufficient. Archived validation MUST NOT lower a live verifier's monotonic trust watermark.

Once the signature commits to a certificate representation, its original bytes MUST remain unchanged. Converting a relative certificate to standalone form does not preserve a commitment to the original encoding. Additional independently validated evidence may be stored only under a declared supplementary binding; this draft does not allocate such a wire format.

The retained set MUST include:

- original content, signature container and certificate representation;
- issuer, cosigner, timestamp and other application-required authority evidence;
- authenticated status history, its coverage and policy decisions for both supplied times;
- required inclusion, consistency and monitoring evidence, including scope;
- time proofs, renewal chains and the evidence needed to validate their authorities.

An unavailable required dependency yields INDETERMINATE. An invalid signature, inconsistent binding or applicable revocation yields INVALID. Required evidence that is stale at the selected time cannot yield VALID. Unsupported revisions fail explicitly; trying a weaker policy is not recovery.

### 3.1. Historical status and authorization coverage

The selected application MUST declare every required history question, including the certificate status and issuer authorization question where applicable. Each question identifies the subject, purpose or log/range scope, admitted source and selected profile through policy obtained independently of the archive. A status response cannot establish issuer appointment or authorization for a later document-signing operation. In particular, an OCSP `good` response alone does not prove that a certificate was issued (RFC 6960, section 2.2).

For each question, the verifier MUST retain the original status or authority record and the evidence needed to authenticate its signer, delegation, applicability and publication history. It MUST resolve whether that source covers the state time and whether its history is complete through the knowledge time. CRL and OCSP `thisUpdate`/`nextUpdate` and OCSP `producedAt` retain their format-specific meanings (RFC 5280, section 5.1.2; RFC 6960, sections 2.4 and 4.2.2). None alone proves that a retained history is complete through an arbitrary later knowledge time. Unknown status, missing source coverage, a stale record, or an unproven knowledge horizon is INDETERMINATE. An applicable authenticated denial or contradictory authenticated facts cannot satisfy the history question. Later discovered compromise is evaluated at the chosen knowledge time under the selected policy, including its effective time and scope.

The reference history function consumes *already authenticated and resolved* facts. Its `stateFrom`/`stateUntil` interval is half-open; its `knowledgeFrom`/`knowledgeThrough` interval is closed and describes the cutoffs for which the decision and completeness have been established. `knowledgeThrough: null` means no such completeness proof. The function requires an exact profile, subject, scope and independently admitted source match. `SATISFIED` means only that the declared history questions have coverage and allow decisions at the two supplied times; it is not document validity, an OCSP/CRL parser, a trust resolver, or a new wire format. A production verifier MUST authenticate raw records, prove the stated coverage and completeness, and apply the selected application's full signature, time, preservation and admissibility checks before returning document VALID.

### 3.2. Original status records and historical coverage

When an application admits [RFC 6960 OCSP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6960) or [RFC 5280 CRLs](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5280) as a status source for a particular certificate, it MUST retain the original signed DER record and independently admitted issuer, responder, trust and source policy. An OCSP observation MUST bind the response to the target issuer name, issuer public key and serial, verify the signed ResponseData and responder identity and authority, and compare a retained request nonce when one was used. A matching serial alone, an embedded responder certificate alone, or OCSP `good` alone does not establish issuance, MTC issuer appointment or permission for the document-signing action. A CRL observation MUST verify the original signed TBSCertList, issuer and target scope; absence of an entry is informative only with all required distribution-point and reason coverage. A later record may carry an earlier compromise-effective time, which the selected application must interpret under an explicit policy.

The bounded raw-status reference accepts one target certificate directly signed by an externally admitted CA certificate, a BasicOCSPResponse with a CA signer or a directly CA-issued responder bearing `id-kp-OCSPSigning` and `id-pkix-ocsp-nocheck`, or a complete direct all-reasons CRL for one URI distribution point with no issuing-distribution-point extension. It supports RSA/SHA-256 OCSP and CRL record signatures and SHA-1 or SHA-256 OCSP CertID hashes; SHA-1 here identifies a certificate and is not a permitted status-record signature algorithm. Other path shapes, responder revocation methods, partitioned or indirect CRLs, delta CRLs, additional distribution points, unsupported cryptography and unknown critical status extensions return explicit unresolved or unsupported results. Production verification MUST apply its selected MTC/PKIX certificate interpretation and critical-extension policy independently; this reference does not validate those certificate semantics or install trust anchors from retained material.

The reference's `stateAt` is the selected historical certificate-state time; `knownAt` is the latest admitted knowledge time. A separately authenticated receipt supplied by the caller binds a record hash, source and observed time. The reference checks that binding and rejects a receipt dated before its record, but it does not authenticate the receipt or prove all status changes were captured. `thisUpdate`, `nextUpdate`, `producedAt`, CRL issue time, revocation time and `invalidityDate` remain separate. The application explicitly chooses whether a retained `invalidityDate` governs retrospective compromise; a later revoked record never becomes a prior `good` record by implication. `nextUpdate`, OCSP `archiveCutoff` and one receipt MUST NOT be promoted to `knowledgeThrough` or to a complete historical status interval. `GOOD`, `REVOKED` and `UNKNOWN` are scoped observations from one authenticated record at the chosen state and knowledge times; `INDETERMINATE`, `UNSUPPORTED` and `REJECTED` describe missing coverage, unsupported form or invalid binding. None is an issuer-authorization fact or a document VALID result. A history resolver may use an observation only after independently proving required source completeness and authority history through `knownAt`.

### 3.3. Original signed authority-source claims

An application MAY select this bounded source-claim profile for issuer appointment and authorization of a particular document action. It is a proposal in this draft, not an MTC, OCSP, CRL or AdES wire object, and `ALLOW` does not represent the signer's intent. Its policy MUST independently bind each `(profileId, role, subjectId, scope, sourceId)` stream to one admitted Ed25519 SubjectPublicKeyInfo key; a key or source name found only inside the archive MUST NOT appoint itself. The `document-action` scope is `sha256:` followed by the lower-case SHA-256 digest of the exact supplied document bytes. The application still has to bind that document, the signer identity and the actual signature container under its selected signing policy. Certificate issuance, OCSP `good`, and this action claim answer different questions.

Each source record is exact UTF-8 compact JSON with the field order and primitive types fixed in the [reference](reference/raw-authority-history.mjs). The common fields are `version:1`, `kind`, `sourceId`, positive `sequence`, `previousSha256`, `profileId`, `role`, `subjectId`, `scope`, and non-negative integer `announcedAt` in Unix seconds. An `event` adds half-open `stateFrom`/`stateUntil` integer seconds and `ALLOW`, `DENY`, or `UNKNOWN`; a `seal` adds `through`, which MUST be no later than `announcedAt`. Strings in this subset are printable ASCII; duplicate keys, alternate encodings and extra fields are rejected. Its detached [RFC 8032](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8032) Ed25519 signature covers the ASCII bytes `MTC-DV-AUTHORITY-HISTORY-v1` followed by NUL and the original JSON bytes, without prehash. From sequence two onward, `previousSha256` is SHA-256 over the ASCII bytes `MTC-DV-AUTHORITY-LINK-v1` followed by NUL, the preceding original JSON bytes and its 64-byte signature; sequence one uses `null`. A verifier MUST verify every admitted signature, the exact stream binding, contiguous sequence and nondecreasing claimed announcement times before relying on a seal. The `seal` claims source coverage through `through`; only a seal claimed announced no later than `knownAt` and covering `knownAt` can support a cutoff claim. A later event MUST NOT be imported into an earlier cutoff. A scoped later `DENY` claim defeats an earlier `ALLOW` claim for that `stateAt` under this profile.

`BOUND_CLAIMS` means only that the admitted keys signed internally linked, scoped claims for both required questions. It is not `SATISFIED` from §3.1 and never document `VALID`. Source-supplied `announcedAt` and `through`, even with valid signatures and no sequence gap, cannot prove trustworthy clocks, the absence of withheld records or source equivocation. The selected application MUST independently establish those matters, the source's appointment and any key rotation, applicable status history, document signature coverage, and archive custody or succession before deriving authenticated facts for §3.1. Missing or stale seals, unknown claims and missing source streams remain INDETERMINATE; invalid signatures, noncanonical bytes and broken links fail explicitly. Retirement of a live service does not relax these requirements.

For a selected `knownAt`, the verifier MUST assess every admitted event whose claimed `announcedAt` is no later than that cutoff, including events later in sequence than a qualifying seal but announced in the same second. An applicable signed `DENY` defeats an earlier `ALLOW` even in that case. A positive claim MUST NOT rely on a seal followed by another admitted event at or before the cutoff; that suffix has not been closed by the selected seal. These are bounds on source-signed claims, not independent evidence that all real events were published.

### 3.4. Original standalone MTC issuance proof

For an original standalone certificate under the selected [MTC revision 07](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-plants-merkle-tree-certs-07.txt), the verifier MUST start with the retained DER certificate and its directly encoded TLS `MTCProof`, not an archive's statement that issuance occurred. The selected application's policy MUST independently admit the exact `mtcRevision`, `logHash`, CA ID, supported CA and cosigner IDs and public keys, required cosigner roles, the CA's allowed `minSerial`/`maxSerial`, and the complete additional revoked-range set. The serial bounds and additional range endpoints are exact decimal strings; additional ranges are half-open and may end at `2^64`. Serial numbers below `minSerial` or above `maxSerial` are rejected even if the additional list is empty; this enforces the mandatory outer ranges from MTC -07 §7.1. The certificate or archive MUST NOT install its own trust policy. In this bounded profile, the CA cosigner and every separately required role must be represented by a valid signature over the reconstructed `CosignedSubtree`; unknown GREASE signatures cannot satisfy a role.

The verifier MUST establish that the retained bytes are a structurally valid X.509 Certificate before reconstructing signed fields; a well-signed hash of malformed validity, public-key algorithm or extension bytes is not enough. An explicitly present X.509 `Extensions` field MUST contain at least one `Extension`; the optional field may be absent. This X.509 cardinality is independent of the TLS `MTCProof` extensions vector, which may be empty. It MUST check both `id-alg-mtcProof` algorithm identifiers without parameters, the CA ID issuer name, serial-derived positive log number and zero-based leaf index, proof framing and ordered extension/cosigner IDs. Every cosigner ID, including one whose signature is ignored as unknown GREASE, MUST have complete, minimally encoded RELATIVE-OID bytes. The full derived binary log ID, formed by appending the log-number arcs to the CA ID, MUST fit the 32-byte [trust-anchor-ID](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-tls-trust-anchor-ids-06.txt) bound, even when the CA ID alone fits. It reconstructs `TBSCertificateLogEntry` from the original covered fields, replacing the subject public key with SHA-256 of the original DER `SubjectPublicKeyInfo`, then hashes the exact TLS `MTCLogEntry` with the `0x00` leaf prefix. It evaluates the inclusion path with SHA-256 and `0x01` internal-node prefixes against the proof's aligned subtree interval. For a certificate-carried signature, the signed `CosignedSubtree` uses the independently admitted signer and log origin, zero timestamp, exact subtree bounds and evaluated root. Invalid parsing, binding, path or required signatures cannot yield `ISSUANCE_PROVEN`.

The [bounded reference](reference/raw-mtc-issuance.mjs) requires policy fields `mtcRevision: "07"`, `logHash: "sha256"`, `representation: "standalone"`, and decimal `minSerial`/`maxSerial`, and supports admitted Ed25519 CA/cosigner keys; its [frozen original bytes](vectors/raw-mtc-der.json) and [positive and negative cases](vectors/raw-mtc-cases.json) define its tested subset. Its X.509 structural parser accepts the default v1 or explicit v3 version field and excludes v2 and unique-ID fields; the frozen positive corpus exercises v3. A successful `ISSUANCE_PROVEN` means only that the admitted CA certified this certificate's reconstructed entry. Each additionally required cosigner signed the same subtree with the meaning of its independently admitted role; a mirror signature does not itself certify the entry. Other MTC algorithms, relative certificates and trusted-landmark routes need a separately specified and tested verifier. This reference does not complete X.509 path and critical-extension checks, historical status and issuer appointment, trusted time, retained-source completeness, document-signature coverage, action authority, archive continuity or document `VALID`. A preserved standalone proof remains useful after issuer retirement only while its independently admitted policy, keys and exact original bytes remain available and applicable.

## 4. Time and protection lifetimes

An authenticated timestamp bounds existence, subject to TSA authority, status and stated accuracy. It does not establish the signer's intention or the exact signing instant. Declared signing time, issuance time and a log clock do not replace that evidence.

For an archive protecting one immutable data object, freeze that object before requesting its first archive timestamp. Later status and authority material may form an augmented version; it MUST NOT rewrite the original object or earlier timestamp input.

Let a timestamp have generation time g, accuracy a, and upper bound u = g + a. The entire interval MUST fit the admitted TSA lifetime and precede or equal evaluation time. Initial archive protection MUST satisfy u less than the original data protection deadline. Ordinary signer-certificate expiry alone is not that deadline.

For consecutive renewals, the previous upper bound MUST be no later than the next lower bound g - a. The next upper bound MUST precede expiry of the previous protection. At evaluation, the final protection MUST still be adequate.

Timestamp renewal preserves the current chain's data hash. Hash renewal replaces that protection and must cover the original object and preceding evidence according to the selected evidence-record format. A later timestamp under an already inadequate data hash cannot repair the intervening gap. For each old hash, the replacing chain's upper bound MUST precede its protection deadline; when no replacement exists, evaluation time MUST precede it.

The reference lifetime function operates only on already authenticated timestamp facts. It does not parse ERS, verify a TSA, or resolve historical trust. A production verifier must perform those checks before using the function.

### 4.1. Original RFC 3161 timestamp claims

When an application admits an [RFC 3161](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3161) timestamp for retained evidence, it MUST preserve the original `TimeStampResp` and CMS token, the exact bytes whose digest the `TSTInfo` commits to, and the original request when a nonce or requested policy is relied on. It MUST verify the response status, CMS signed attributes and signature over their DER `SET OF` encoding, the encapsulated `TSTInfo` digest, signer certificate identity, the `messageImprint`, requested policy and nonce. When the request sets `certReq`, the signer certificate MUST be included in the response; otherwise an independently admitted copy may be used. A certificate included in the token is a candidate key, not an installed trust anchor. The TSA path, timestamp purpose, applicable status and compromise history, trust policy and clock or receipt basis are admitted independently of the archive.

When the retained request omits `certReq` or sets it to false, [RFC 3161 §2.4.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3161#section-2.4.1) requires the response SignedData `certificates` field to be absent, including an otherwise empty field. A DER-encoded request with the DEFAULT FALSE value explicitly encoded is noncanonical and rejected by this bounded reference. When no original request is retained, the verifier cannot infer which `certReq` value the requester sent; it does not assert request compliance from the response alone.

The bounded reference supports one RSA/SHA-256 CMS signer, a SHA-256 imprint, a directly CA-issued TSA certificate with a critical sole time-stamping EKU, and the SHA-256 `ESSCertIDv2` signer binding of [RFC 5816](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5816) and [RFC 5035](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5035). It checks CMS `content-type` and `message-digest` according to [RFC 5652](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5652). Legacy `ESSCertID` and dual ESS attributes are explicit unsupported forms in this subset; a verifier MUST NOT ignore a second, potentially conflicting signer identifier. An absent `accuracy` is not zero accuracy; unsupported algorithms, certificate paths and valid CMS forms fail explicitly. Production validation MUST process the applicable PKIX critical extensions and historical TSA certificate status beyond this bounded direct-signature reference. A successfully checked token yields `SIGNED_CLAIM` with `genTime`, explicit accuracy and a *claimed* upper bound as exact decimal microsecond integers, not an authenticated proof-of-existence bound. A verifier MUST use exact or conservatively rounded interval arithmetic at subsecond protection deadlines. Only after the selected application separately resolves TSA status, authority, time reliability and the relevant original object coverage may it use that interval as an authenticated fact in the lifetime rules. A timestamp alone does not prove signer intent, document authorization, full ERS continuity or document VALID.

### 4.2. Original binary ERS binding

For a detached [RFC 4998](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4998) EvidenceRecord, the application MUST identify and freeze the exact archived byte stream before the initial ArchiveTimeStamp. A later augmented package, a reserialized signature container and the original signed object are different byte streams. The verifier MUST retain the original DER EvidenceRecord, its immutable archived object, each embedded `timeStamp` CMS `ContentInfo`, and the original RFC 3161 request when its nonce or requested policy is relied on. An ERS `timeStamp` is a token, not a `TimeStampResp`; validation of a bare token MUST NOT infer a response status or request binding that the retained inputs do not show.

The selected one-object binary subset uses v1 ASN.1 ERS, SHA-256 for the data/tree and timestamp imprint, and the bounded RFC 3161 token checks in §4.1. It MUST verify the declared digest-algorithm union, each original token signature and imprint, and every reduced-tree level. The first `PartialHashtree` contains the object's digest; its hashes are binary sorted at full width. At each higher level the computed parent is inserted virtually among the encoded sibling hashes, sorted and hashed. A record without a reduced tree timestamps the one input directly. Membership of one archived object does not establish that a group contains only that object. The reference reports encrypted records, unhandled ATS attributes, other algorithms and timestamp types as unsupported; `cryptoInfos` cannot appoint an anchor or authenticate historical status.

Within a chain, each later ArchiveTimeStamp MUST bind the complete original DER `ContentInfo` of the preceding `timeStamp`, rather than the preceding whole ArchiveTimeStamp or the original document again. For the first ArchiveTimeStamp of a new chain, this draft selects the numbered construction in RFC 4998 §5.2: `H(H(d) || H(P))`, where `d` is the unchanged archived object and `P` is the complete DER `ArchiveTimeStampSequence` of all preceding chains, including its outer tag and length. RFC 4998 Figure 4 instead describes binary sorting of the two inner hashes. This profile makes the choice explicit and MUST NOT try both until one matches; a validly signed record using that alternate pair order is `UNSUPPORTED` by this subset, not a claim that RFC 4998 itself is invalid. Bare concatenation of chain encodings and omission of earlier chains fail binding. XMLERS under [RFC 6283](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6283) has separate syntax and processing.

The reference returns `BOUND_CLAIMS` only when the original object, reduced trees, predecessor bytes, signed tokens and claimed time order bind. It marks absent original requests as `NOT_PROVIDED`. These are signed claims, not authenticated preservation continuity or document `VALID`: the selected application MUST separately establish historical TSA path and status, clock reliability, algorithm-protection deadlines, timely renewal, final protection at evaluation, actual document signature coverage, MTC authority/status history and present admissibility before passing authenticated facts to the lifetime rules.

## 5. Monitoring, retirement and archive transfer

Issuance monitoring checks issued certificates, log consistency, operator policy and equivocation under the selected MTC composition. It cannot observe arbitrary later signatures produced with an existing private key. Document-operation monitoring requires separately justified application evidence.

The selected historical deployment use-cases revision 00, section 3.5.5, proposed keeping every published batch tree head available at least through the maximum certificate validity in its batch, including after CA retirement, on infrastructure separate from the live CA. Revision 01 instead calls for an archival plan for log data and published heads over an appropriate period after decommissioning; it does not state that numeric floor or infrastructure rule. An application using the following batch-head availability check independently selects the historical per-batch floor as its retention policy. A longer document-validation horizon can require longer retention.

For an application that selects this batch-head retention policy, a post-retirement batch-head check MUST use an independently authenticated inventory of every head published through issuer retirement, including the original bytes and an admitted minimum-access end for each batch. The verifier MUST check current retrieval from an independently admitted archive source for every head still within its minimum. An inventory supplied only by the successor, a former issuing endpoint, or a past retrieval does not establish current archive availability. If inventory completeness through retirement is unproved, or a required head cannot currently be retrieved, that check is INDETERMINATE; an authenticated byte mismatch rejects the byte-identity claim. These conclusions are separate from the validity of one selected document.

The selected application's document closure is checked against its separately admitted, complete list of required original bytes through its declared validation horizon. All heads can be accessible while a required status or authorization record is missing; a particular document's retained evidence can be accessible while an unrelated published head is absent. Missing or stale retrieval is INDETERMINATE for the affected check, and an authenticated mismatch rejects that byte-identity claim. End of a batch-head minimum does not shorten a longer document horizon. OUT_OF_SCOPE means only that the particular stated period has ended, not that material needed by another obligation may be discarded.

The reference archive-availability function consumes already authenticated inventory, baseline bytes, source attribution, retirement and retrieval facts under policy obtained independently of the archive. Its `completeThrough`, per-head `floorThrough` and `documentThrough` endpoints are inclusive; an observation counts only at the reported check time. `SATISFIED` is limited to the declared byte set and that time. The function does not authenticate the inventory or source independence, prove that an application's required list is complete, verify MTC or ERS, establish custody continuity, predict future access, or return document VALID. Its hexadecimal fact representation is a test interface, not an archive or transfer wire format.

An archive policy MUST state the document validation horizon, responsible custodians, retrieval obligations, status/authority-history sources, algorithm review schedule and succession process. Retention cannot end solely because the signing certificate expired.

Before an issuer or archive operator retires, the successor obtains the retained closure, verifies byte identity and completeness, and records responsibility and access continuity through the deployment's existing administrative mechanism. Partial transfer must not be reported as completed preservation. Transfer does not install new trust anchors or erase earlier compromise information.

Retirement alone does not cancel an issuer authorization that applied at the selected state time. The successor MUST retain enough authenticated appointment, status and change history to decide that earlier time at a later knowledge cutoff. A later applicable withdrawal or compromise can change the decision; an unavailable successor record cannot be treated as proof that no change occurred.

For each change of custodian during the declared document validation horizon, a transfer-continuity check MUST start from an independently admitted complete inventory of the original object and all required prior evidence, including earlier evidence records and historical status material. As new evidence is created, the inventory grows; each handoff must cover every required item already created by its acceptance time. The admitted handoff facts MUST identify the predecessor, successor, acceptance time, retained-item scope and responsibility end. Acceptance after the predecessor's admitted responsibility end leaves a gap; the final recorded custodian's responsibility MUST extend through the document horizon. The verifier MUST separately establish the successor's possession of the inventory's original bytes at each acceptance time. An omitted item, missing observation, uncovered period or broken predecessor chain yields INDETERMINATE for the affected claim; an authenticated byte mismatch rejects the transfer byte-identity claim. A handoff receipt alone cannot establish that the bytes were received.

The reference transfer-continuity function evaluates an externally authenticated inventory, handoff sequence and byte observations; it reports recorded responsibility and observed transfer-time evidence separately. Its synthetic hexadecimal facts are not a receipt or archive wire format. The function does not authenticate records, establish a trust anchor, prove current or future access, verify ERS renewal, or return document VALID. The transfer requirement is this draft's application profile built on the transfer capability in informational RFC 4810 §4.6, not a new MTC protocol rule.

## 7. Security and evidence

Adversarial cases include missing proof material, archive-supplied trust, inconsistent log scope, stale status, retrospective compromise, late renewal, hash-protection gaps, timestamp interval overlap and cross-profile substitution. Availability and privacy of historical lookups remain deployment properties.

The positive and negative cases and reference tests cover ordering, protection deadlines, original scoped status and timestamp records, ERS binding, signed authority-source claims and decisions over already authenticated history facts. Complete historical source resolution, independently established coverage and clock evidence, actual archive succession and all AdES container augmentation require additional implementation evidence. Independent adoption is possible without a particular authority hierarchy or wallet ecosystem.
