# MTC Document Validation

Long-term validation and preservation requirements for documents signed using Merkle Tree Certificates, including evidence closure, independent monitoring and issuer retirement.

**Draft 01. No stable release or upstream adoption is claimed.**

[Draft](draft.md) defines the technical scope and limits. [Sources](sources.json) pins the upstream documents. [Cases](cases.json) identifies the positive and negative evaluation cases.

The [history vectors](vectors/history.json) constrain a fact-only decision over already authenticated status and issuer-authorization facts. `SATISFIED` is a history precondition, not a document-validation result. This fact resolver does not itself verify the raw records, archive completeness or AdES containers.

This repository can be reviewed and used independently of the CertConcord framework. It does not require the framework's authority hierarchy, operation objects or deployment. The framework selects this work through a pinned dependency and adds its own integration policy.

The [archive-availability vectors](vectors/archive-availability.json) exercise separate post-retirement checks for an admitted published-head inventory and the selected document's declared evidence set. The reference consumes independently admitted facts and reports byte availability at one check time; it does not establish complete document validity or future access.

The [transfer-continuity vectors](vectors/transfer-continuity.json) test two successive custodians against an admitted original-object and prior-evidence inventory. Recorded responsibility and observed transfer-time bytes are separate results; neither proves present or future retrieval.

The [original signed transfer corpus](vectors/raw-transfer-claims.json) and [raw reference](reference/raw-transfer-claims.mjs) apply a separate CertConcord application profile to the same 23 original document-evidence items. Two custodians sign each handoff's exact bytes, and a distinct observer signs per-item digest claims. The reference checks the preselected-set anchor, signatures, chain, scope, claimed times and supplied original bytes, reporting responsibility and observation claims separately. A later retirement declaration cannot establish that the set was selected before the first handoff. These bounded claims do not prove physical transfer, trustworthy time, complete source history, future retrieval or document validity.

The [successor-set original corpus](vectors/raw-transfer-set-evolution.json) and [replay](reference/raw-transfer-set-evolution.test.mjs) extend those claims across one independently admitted 23-to-24 item set update. The later signed retirement manifest enters the second handoff's required originals while the first handoff remains bound to its earlier set. Original bytes, old rows, exact set linkage, signatures, selection order and missing or substituted evidence are checked separately. This remains a two-handoff application subset, not an IETF transfer format or evidence of physical custody, complete history, trusted time or document validity.

The [frozen DER corpus](vectors/raw-status-der.json) and [positive and negative cases](vectors/raw-status-cases.json) constrain a narrow [raw-status reference](reference/raw-status.mjs) for RFC 6960 OCSP and RFC 5280 direct CRLs. It checks original signed bytes and their target, signer, request, scope and time bindings under externally admitted policy and receipt facts, including rejection if any signed CRL entry claims a revocation date later than CRL issuance. A scoped `GOOD` observation does not prove certificate issuance, MTC issuer authorization, a complete history through the knowledge cutoff, or document validity. Unsupported CRL forms and cryptography fail explicitly.

The [MTC target and direct status DER corpus](vectors/raw-mtc-status-der.json) and [paired cases](vectors/raw-mtc-status-cases.json) add an explicit [MTC status admission path](reference/raw-status.mjs) for the selected -07 revision. It first verifies the exact target's standalone MTCProof, binds an independently admitted MTC CA representation to the CA cosigner key and serial range, then checks direct Ed25519 CA-signed OCSP or CRL bytes for that target. A formerly positive signed CRL with a future target revocation date is now a rejection control; a separately signed same-second CRL and its own target proof retain the positive revoked path. The synthetic CA certificates are signed by separate superior keys; production path admission remains external. Explicit responder certificates, including delegated OCSP, and other status signature suites remain outside this path. The frozen responder-input case supplies the same CA DER, so it does not claim to exercise a distinct delegation. `GOOD` stays one scoped status observation, not complete historical coverage or document validity.

The [frozen timestamp corpus](vectors/raw-timestamp-der.json) and [positive and negative cases](vectors/raw-timestamp-cases.json) constrain a bounded [timestamp reference](reference/raw-timestamp.mjs). It contains original DER records and exact covered bytes, and verifies a signed TSA claim about those bytes, including CMS attributes, ESS certificate identity and the original request when retained. `SIGNED_CLAIM` does not establish historical TSA status, clock reliability, signature coverage, full preservation continuity or document validity; those require independent application evidence.

The [same-TSA retirement CRLs](vectors/raw-tsa-retirement.json) and [reference](reference/raw-tsa-retirement.mjs) compare original signed RFC 3161 responses with sixteen independently signed CA CRL alternatives for that exact TSA certificate. This narrowly selected application path handles a certificate without a CRL distribution point only with an explicitly admitted direct issuer-wide source and separately admitted receipt. It distinguishes pre-revocation cessation, other permitted non-compromise reasons, key compromise, omitted reason code, overlapping accuracy, a signed earlier invalidity date, illegal version omission with extensions, a revocation date after CRL issuance on the target or another entry, unadmitted source or receipt inputs, and tampered bytes. `BOUNDED_PRE_REVOCATION_CLAIM` does not prove complete status history, trusted time, current token validity or document validity.

The [original binary ERS corpus](vectors/raw-ers-der.json) and [binding cases](vectors/raw-ers-cases.json) constrain a detached, one-object [ERS reference](reference/raw-ers.mjs). It checks original RFC 4998 DER, reduced trees, exact preceding timestamp bytes and data plus prior-chain hash renewal using the signed CMS tokens embedded in the record. `BOUND_CLAIMS` remains subject to independently authenticated TSA history, algorithm protection, renewal timing and document-signature validation.

The [exact preservation-time cases](vectors/lifetime-microseconds.json) replay original signed ERS tokens and then test admitted microsecond interval and deadline facts through the [lifetime reference](reference/lifetimes.mjs). The microsecond entry uses decimal strings and integer arithmetic; its compatibility seconds entry accepts safe whole seconds only. A signed timestamp claim is not by itself an authenticated protection interval, and a passing lifetime check is not document validity.

The [original signed authority corpus](vectors/raw-authority-bytes.json) and [cases](vectors/raw-authority-cases.json) constrain a bounded [authority-history reference](reference/raw-authority-history.mjs). It checks exact source-signed appointment and document-action statements, source sequence links, claimed knowledge cutoffs and the exact document hash. `BOUND_CLAIMS` authenticates only those source claims and their internal binding. The application must independently admit each source and establish trustworthy time, actual source completeness, signer-to-document binding and succession before using them as historical facts or returning document validity.

The [original publication witness corpus](vectors/raw-publication-witness.json) and [cases](vectors/raw-publication-witness-cases.json) constrain a separate [log-prefix reference](reference/raw-publication-witness.mjs). It checks original entries against a signed application head, an independently admitted earlier anchor, a consistency proof, and a separately signed monitor observation. `MONITORED_STREAM_THROUGH` covers only the logged prefix at that head. The valid four-entry vector deliberately omits a fifth source-signed action: a monitor cannot infer an unsubmitted event, and this result never establishes complete real-world history or document validity.

The [original signed retirement corpus](vectors/raw-retirement-closure.json) and [cases](vectors/raw-retirement-closure-cases.json) constrain a [declared-inventory and retrieval-claim reference](reference/raw-retirement-closure.mjs). It binds an independently signed inventory to a verified log prefix and original document, certificate, status, authority and preservation bytes, then checks signed archive claims against supplied bytes at one check second. `BOUND_RETIREMENT_RETRIEVAL_CLAIMS` does not prove that the archive actually served the bytes then, that its list is globally complete, or that the document is valid. A signed four-entry claimed-final inventory remains bounded despite the unsubmitted fifth same-second `DENY`.

The [coherent original evidence corpus](vectors/coherent-document-evidence.json) and [cross-evidence replay](reference/coherent-document-evidence.test.mjs) connect one document and its detached signature to the MTC signer, direct CA status, source-signed authorization, RFC 3161 claims, two RFC 4998 records, and a 23-item signed retirement inventory. Cross-object substitutions, missing bytes and receipts, and a same-second unsubmitted `DENY` are negative controls. Passing these bounded checks does not authenticate external policy admission, prove complete status or authorization streams, establish a trusted clock or actual archive service, or yield document `VALID`.

The [same-document ERS renewal originals](vectors/coherent-ers-renewal.json) and [replay](reference/coherent-ers-renewal.test.mjs) retain that document and its first ArchiveTimeStamp byte for byte. A second RFC 3161 response signs the exact previous CMS `ContentInfo`; a separately signed response over the whole previous ArchiveTimeStamp is rejected, as is substitution of the original document. These checks establish signed byte binding and claimed time order only. The frozen custodian handoffs do not establish that this renewal occurred between transfers.

The [original MTC certificate corpus](vectors/raw-mtc-der.json) and [issuance cases](vectors/raw-mtc-cases.json) constrain a bounded [standalone MTC proof reference](reference/raw-mtc-issuance.mjs) under the selected revision 07. It checks the original X.509 DER structure and TLS proof bytes, reconstructed log-entry and subtree hashes, admitted CA serial bounds, and CA and required cosigner signatures. `ISSUANCE_PROVEN` is only the certificate-signature step; X.509 path, historical status and authority, document signature, trusted time and preservation remain separate.

The [one-leaf original relative corpus](vectors/raw-mtc-relative.json), [multi-leaf direct corpus](vectors/raw-mtc-relative-multileaf.json) and their [one-leaf](vectors/raw-mtc-relative-cases.json) and [multi-leaf](vectors/raw-mtc-relative-multileaf-cases.json) cases constrain a bounded [landmark-relative issuance reference](reference/raw-mtc-relative.mjs). It verifies the selected subtree against a directly cosigned checkpoint through an original MTC consistency proof, with publication identity, log scope and minimum tree size admitted outside the archive. The multi-leaf positive connects nonempty certificate and consistency paths to a distinct signed root. Unadmitted extra checkpoint signatures cannot supply a required role or veto the admitted set. `RELATIVE_ISSUANCE_BOUND` is an issuance-provenance result, not document validity. Other algorithm suites remain outside the reference.

The same reference also accepts each required role's original signature on a later containing checkpoint when an [RFC 9162 consistency proof](reference/raw-mtc-log-consistency.mjs) connects the authenticated reference root to that signed later root. Its [seven-entry original corpus](vectors/raw-mtc-later-checkpoint.json), [composition cases](vectors/raw-mtc-later-checkpoint-cases.json) and [ordered prefix-proof cases](vectors/raw-mtc-log-consistency.json) cover direct and later signatures, distinct later sizes, and failed signature, log, root, size and proof bindings. This adds an append-only checkpoint edge under the selected MTC -07 profile; it does not establish trusted time, all published checkpoints, historical status or document validity.

The [active-subtree-set reference](reference/raw-mtc-active-subtrees.mjs) checks both canonical subtrees of every selected active landmark against one authenticated reference checkpoint. Its [original publication, log and proof corpus](vectors/raw-mtc-active-subtree-set.json) and [positive and negative cases](vectors/raw-mtc-active-subtree-cases.json) distinguish a complete set relative to one independently admitted publication from an explicitly declared older contiguous suffix. A same-landmark publication refresh may shorten the file after older rows expire; the reference checks retained row identity against the original admitted prior view. A canonical empty subtree is retained but cannot contain a certificate. Publication source and freshness, per-log watermark and a CA-wide cap allocation must be admitted independently; these results do not prove a current global publication inventory or document validity.
