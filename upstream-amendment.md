# Retention horizon clarification for MTC signed artifacts

Target: [draft-ietf-plants-mtc-deploy-use-cases-01, section 3.5.5](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-ietf-plants-mtc-deploy-use-cases-01.txt). Status as of 26 November 2026: related, narrower archival-plan wording was submitted for discussion in [upstream Draft PR #20](https://github.com/ietf-plants-wg/deploy-use-cases/pull/20), which remains open and unmerged. The example and proposed addition below are this component's own wording, not the text submitted in that PR. Neither the upstream PR nor this application profile is claimed to have been adopted by the WG.

Revision 00 proposed a specific minimum for every published batch tree head and access after CA retirement. Revision 01 instead asks for an archival plan covering log data and published heads for an appropriate period after decommissioning; it no longer states that numeric minimum or a separate-infrastructure rule. This proposed addition addresses how a deployment assigns responsibility when an application needs longer document validation. A deployment may select the older minimum as its own policy, but revision 01 does not impose it.

For example, a signing certificate expires in 2030, but a document signed while it was authorized must remain verifiable through 2045. Assume a deployment chose to retain the corresponding batch heads through 2030. Stopping all retention then would leave the application without required evidence for 2031-2045. The certificate expiry date alone does not establish when a selected retention policy has been satisfied. Continued access to batch tree heads alone does not supply the document profile's historical status, authority and time evidence. A positive deployment declares the 2045 horizon, names the parties retaining those inputs, and arranges retrieval and any necessary protection renewal throughout that period. Certificate expiry alone is not evidence that an already preserved signature became invalid.

Proposed addition:

> For signed artifacts whose intended validation horizon extends beyond signing-certificate validity, a deployment needs an explicit retention period and responsible archive parties. Retained verification inputs include certificate/proof material and the authority, status and time evidence required by the selected application. Certificate expiry alone does not terminate that responsibility. Issuer retirement needs a continuity arrangement for those inputs.

This addition is informative deployment guidance. Exact container augmentation, policy decisions and preservation algorithms belong to application profiles, including the accompanying document-validation draft. It does not propose another MTC certificate type or make every artifact deployment use one trust framework. Revision 01 mentions an optional artifact-associated receipt to identify a log entry but does not define a code-signing receipt wire object; such a reference is not evidence of a later document-signing operation.
